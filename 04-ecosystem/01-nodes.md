# Nodes

Nodes are the primary unit of work in ARGC. Each node is a small, focused group of engineers with a defined domain — platform, evaluations, events, or outreach. You belong to a node before you belong to ARGC, and your node's output is your accountability before anything else.

## What a Node Is

A node is not a team in the casual sense. It is an accountable unit with a defined scope of responsibility, a leader who owns that scope, and members whose contribution is tracked within it.

Every node has:
- A domain (what the node is responsible for)
- A Node Leader (who carries accountability for the domain's output)
- Members (who contribute within the domain and accumulate XP doing so)
- A contribution record (visible on the platform)

## Why Small

Nodes are kept small intentionally. The right size is large enough to cover the domain, small enough that every member is visible.

When groups get large, contribution becomes diffuse. Individual accountability dilutes into collective ambiguity. The node structure prevents this: in a node of four to six people, there is no hiding.

## Current Node Domains

Nodes are configured by Super Peers based on the club's current operational needs. The stable domains are:

| Domain | What It Covers |
|--------|----------------|
| **Platform** | Building and maintaining the ARGC web platform |
| **Evaluations** | Coordinating and tracking peer evaluation schedules |
| **Events** | Planning and running knowledge sessions, hackathons, and club events |
| **Outreach** | External communications, partnerships, and community presence |

Node domains may be added, split, or merged as the club evolves. Changes require a Super Peer decision.

## How Nodes Interact

Nodes are independent units, not silos. Cross-node collaboration is expected and rewarded with XP.

Cross-node work must be coordinated through Node Leaders, not informally. If a member of one node wants to contribute to another node's output, they raise it with both Node Leaders first. Work done outside that process does not count toward XP.

## Node Accountability

A node that consistently underperforms is not left to drift. Super Peers review node-level contribution data at the end of each cycle. A node below threshold will have its structure reviewed — which may mean leadership change, member reassignment, or consolidation with another node.
