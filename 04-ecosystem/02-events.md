# Events

ARGC events are contribution opportunities, not social gatherings. They exist to produce output: knowledge transfer, peer connection, or external visibility for the club. Members at higher tiers are expected to organize them — planning, facilitation, and follow-through are all tracked and reflected in XP.

## What Makes Something an ARGC Event

An ARGC event has:
- A defined purpose (what it is producing)
- An organizer who owns it from planning to follow-up
- A record — attendees, outcomes, and any artifacts produced

A meetup with no record is not an ARGC event. It is a social gathering that happened to include ARGC members.

## Types of Events

**Knowledge Sessions** — structured internal sessions where a member teaches a specific topic to others. Attendance-limited. Outcome-focused.

**Hackathons** — time-boxed, output-focused build events. Participants ship something real or produce the foundation for something that ships.

**External Events** — presentations, collaborations, or public outputs that involve audiences outside 42 Amman. Require Super Peer sign-off before being organized under the ARGC name.

**Node Sync** — internal operational meetings at the node level. Not events in the public sense, but tracked as contribution activity for the organizer.

## How Events Are Run

1. **Proposal** — submitted in writing to the Node Leader (or Super Peers for external events): what, when, who is organizing, what the deliverable is.
2. **Approval** — Node Leader signs off for internal events. Super Peers sign off for anything external or club-wide.
3. **Execution** — the organizer owns the logistics end-to-end.
4. **Record** — within 48 hours of the event: attendance list, brief outcome summary, any artifacts (notes, code, slides) pushed to the appropriate repo or platform record.

No record = no XP credit for the organizer.

## Why Organizers Get More XP

Organizing is harder than attending. It requires planning, communication, and follow-through. ARGC weights it accordingly.

Members who consistently organize events and document them well are among the highest contributors in any given cycle. Members who only attend are not.
