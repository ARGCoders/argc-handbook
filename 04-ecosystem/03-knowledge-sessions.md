# Knowledge Sessions

Knowledge sessions are structured internal sessions where members teach what they know to members who need it. They are not lectures — they are targeted, applied, and followed by practice. A session that produces no measurable improvement in the participants was not a knowledge session; it was a presentation.

## The Purpose

42 Amman's model produces strong individual engineers. What it does not always produce is knowledge that travels — skills that one person masters but never systematically transfers to others.

Knowledge sessions are ARGC's answer to that gap. They exist to make individual expertise collective property.

## Format

A knowledge session is:
- **Focused** — one topic, one session. Not a survey of a domain.
- **Applied** — the session ends with participants doing something, not just listening.
- **Small** — ideally 4–8 participants. Large enough to generate questions; small enough to be interactive.
- **Documented** — notes, code examples, or a brief write-up pushed to the knowledge base within 24 hours.

What it is not: a presentation to a passive audience, a lecture series, or a showcase for the facilitator.

## How to Run One

1. **Identify the topic** — something you know that others in the club would benefit from learning. Be specific: not "algorithms," but "how I solved the push\_swap problem in under 10ms."
2. **Propose it** — write a one-paragraph description and submit it to your Node Leader. Include the target audience, estimated duration, and what participants will be able to do afterward.
3. **Run it** — keep it under 90 minutes. Spend at least a third of the time on hands-on work.
4. **Document it** — within 24 hours, push notes or code to the knowledge base. Tag the participants.

## What Good Looks Like

A good knowledge session produces a specific, observable outcome: a participant who could not do something before the session can do it after. Ideally they can also explain it to someone else.

If you ran a session and none of the participants improved, the session failed — regardless of how well you prepared. Revise the format and run it again.

## XP

The facilitator earns standard-to-high XP depending on session scope and documentation quality. Participants earn XP for attendance if they complete the associated practice task. Participation without the practice task is not credited.
