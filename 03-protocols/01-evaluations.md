# Evaluations

Peer evaluations are the primary contribution mechanism in ARGC. They are scheduled, documented, and tied directly to XP — missing one without notice is a failure of commitment, not a scheduling issue. Evaluations must be honest: inflated scores corrupt the data that the entire advancement system depends on.

## What an Evaluation Is

A peer evaluation in ARGC context is a structured review of another member's project work at 42 Amman. You review their code, test their implementation, and deliver a score and written feedback.

This is not a favor. It is the core of how 42 Amman's model functions, and ARGC exists in part to make that function at a higher standard than the default.

## The Rules

**Show up.** A scheduled evaluation is a commitment. Missing it without at least 2 hours' notice is recorded as a missed commitment, not a rescheduled one.

**Be honest.** The score you give reflects what you actually observed. A passing score for work that did not pass is a lie that harms the person you evaluated, the people who depend on the XP data, and the club's credibility.

**Be specific.** Feedback that says "good job" or "needs improvement" is not feedback. Write what you saw, what worked, and what did not — in terms the evaluatee can act on.

**Finish the record.** The evaluation is not complete until it is logged on the platform. A verbal evaluation that is not recorded did not happen.

## How Scoring Works

Evaluations contribute to XP based on:
- Completion (showing up and completing the evaluation)
- Honesty signal (cross-referenced over time against peer averages — systematic inflation is detectable)
- Feedback quality (assessed at review cycles by Node Leaders)

You do not get to know your honesty signal in real time. You get to see it reflected in your tier over time.

## What Happens After

Completed evaluations are logged on the platform. Node Leaders review evaluation records as part of the contribution cycle. Patterns of missed evaluations, inflated scores, or incomplete records are flagged and addressed directly.

A strong evaluation record is the clearest signal of what kind of member you are.
