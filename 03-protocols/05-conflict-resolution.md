# Conflict Resolution

Conflicts in ARGC are resolved through a defined escalation path, not through informal pressure or avoidance. Member-level disputes are handled first within the node; unresolved issues escalate to the Node Leader, then to Super Peers. The goal is diagnosis and resolution, not punishment — but unresolved conflicts that affect output are treated as operational failures.

## The Principle

Conflicts are information. They signal a misalignment in expectations, communication, or behavior. The goal of resolution is to close that misalignment, not to assign blame.

What ARGC does not tolerate: conflicts that are allowed to fester because no one wants the discomfort of addressing them. Avoidance is not neutrality — it is a choice that degrades the collective.

## What Counts as a Conflict

A conflict in ARGC is any sustained disagreement or behavioral pattern that:
- Affects another member's ability to contribute
- Undermines trust within a node
- Involves a dispute about evaluation scores or XP records
- Relates to a perceived violation of ARGC's values or code of conduct

Ordinary disagreements resolved between members do not need to be escalated. Use judgment. If you are unsure whether something qualifies, ask your Node Leader.

## The Escalation Path

**Step 1 — Direct resolution**
The parties involved attempt to resolve the conflict directly, in writing. One conversation. If it resolves, it is documented briefly and closed.

**Step 2 — Node Leader**
If direct resolution fails or the conflict involves an evaluation or contribution record, the Node Leader is brought in. The Node Leader reviews the written record and facilitates a resolution. Their decision is binding within the node.

**Step 3 — Super Peers**
If the conflict cannot be resolved at the node level, or if the Node Leader is a party to the conflict, it escalates to Super Peers. Super Peers review the full written record, may speak to parties individually, and issue a final decision.

**There is no Step 4.** Super Peer decisions on conflict are final.

## What Resolution Looks Like

Resolution does not always mean both parties are satisfied. It means the issue has a documented outcome and the club can move forward.

Outcomes may include: acknowledgment of a miscommunication, correction of a platform record, a formal warning, a tier change, or removal from the club. The severity of the outcome matches the severity of the conflict.

## What Is Not Conflict Resolution

- Group chat pressure
- Unofficial "decisions" made in person without a written record
- Waiting for the other person to leave the club

If you are using any of these, you are not resolving a conflict. You are deferring it.
