# Onboarding

Onboarding into ARGC is not an orientation — it is a calibration. New members are assigned to a node, introduced to the evaluation system, and expected to begin contributing immediately. The first 30 days are the signal: members who engage from day one rarely stall; members who wait rarely recover.

## What You Are Walking Into

By the time you are admitted, you have already demonstrated the baseline. ARGC does not need to teach you what contribution looks like — it needs to route your contribution into the right structure.

Onboarding is about two things:
1. Understanding how ARGC's systems work (XP, evaluations, node accountability)
2. Getting your first contribution on record as fast as possible

## Your First Week

- You are assigned to a node. Read the node's current state before your first interaction with it.
- You are given access to the member platform. Understand what it tracks before you ask questions about it.
- You schedule your first peer evaluation within the week. Do not wait to be prompted.
- You introduce yourself to your Node Leader — not with a message, with a task completed.

## The First 30 Days

The first month is a probationary window. Not formal, not punitive — but real. It is the window where the pattern forms.

What the data consistently shows: members who complete at least two evaluations, attend at least one event, and communicate at least once in writing within 30 days sustain their contribution long-term. Members who do none of those things in the first 30 days rarely reach steady state.

No one will chase you. The expectation is that you arrived because you wanted to be here.

## What Stalls New Members

The most common failure mode is waiting for permission. Waiting to be told when to evaluate. Waiting for an event to be announced. Waiting for the Node Leader to reach out first.

ARGC does not operate on permission culture. If you see something that needs doing, you do it. If you are unsure what needs doing, you ask — once, in writing, with a specific question.

## After Onboarding

There is no formal graduation from onboarding. The transition is behavioral: when you stop thinking about what is expected of you and start tracking what your node needs from you, you have arrived.
