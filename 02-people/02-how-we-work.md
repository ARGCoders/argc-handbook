# How We Work

ARGC operates at a higher standard than the default. This section documents what that standard looks like in practice.

## Contribution Is the Job

Being a member is not a title. It is an ongoing commitment to produce output that benefits the collective.

Presence without contribution is not membership — it is occupation. Members who stop contributing are reviewed and may be removed from their tier or from the club.

## Evaluations

Peer evaluations are the primary contribution mechanism for most members. They are:

- **Scheduled, not optional.** A missed evaluation without notice is a failure of commitment, not a scheduling conflict.
- **Honest, not social.** Evaluations reflect what was observed, not what the other person wants to hear. Inflated scores harm the collective.
- **Documented.** Evaluation records are tracked on the platform and contribute directly to XP and advancement.

## Events

Members are expected to participate in club events and, at higher tiers, to organize them. Events are not social gatherings — they are contribution opportunities. Planning, facilitation, and follow-through are all tracked.

## Communication

- Default to written, asynchronous communication for anything that can wait.
- Node leaders are responsible for the information flow within their node.
- Cross-node communication goes through established channels, not informal threads.
- Decisions that affect the club are documented. Verbal agreements are not club policy.

## Code and Platform Work

Work on the ARGC platform follows the same standards as any production system:

- Conventional Commits format for all commits.
- No force-pushes to main. No bypassing review.
- The frontend never calls PocketBase directly. All requests go through the Next.js proxy layer.
- Half-done is not merged. If it is not ready, it stays in a branch.

## Accountability

Every node is accountable for its output. Node leaders carry that accountability upward. Super Peers carry it for the club.

When something fails, we diagnose the system — not just the person. But if a person is consistently the point of failure, that is addressed directly.
