# Membership

Membership in ARGC is not open enrollment — it is earned. Candidates are evaluated on two dimensions: demonstrated technical output within 42 Amman, and a visible track record of helping others before being considered. The club is intentionally small because quality of contribution matters more than size of roster.

## What Membership Is

Membership is an ongoing commitment, not a status. Being accepted does not mean you have arrived — it means you have been recognized as someone who is already doing the work, and you are now expected to keep doing it inside a structure that amplifies it.

There is no honorary membership. There is no passive membership. If you are a member, you are contributing.

## How You Get In

Membership is not applied for through a form. It is recognized through observed behavior.

The criteria:
1. **Technical output** — a demonstrable history of 42 project completion and honest peer evaluation performance.
2. **Contribution disposition** — visible evidence that you help others without being asked. This is not measured by one evaluation. It is a pattern.

When those criteria are met, an existing member or Node Leader may nominate a candidate. The nomination is reviewed by Super Peers. Admission requires consensus, not majority.

## What Is Expected of Members

- Complete peer evaluations on schedule.
- Attend club events and, at higher tiers, organize them.
- Communicate in writing for anything that affects others.
- Treat the node's output as a shared accountability.

Members who meet these expectations advance. Members who do not are reviewed.

## How You Stay

Membership is not permanent. It is renewed through continued contribution.

The review cycle checks XP accumulation, evaluation completion rate, and event participation. Members below threshold are notified. Members who remain below threshold after a review period are removed from their tier — and in cases of sustained absence, from the club.

Leaving ARGC on good terms is respected. Drifting out without notice is not.
