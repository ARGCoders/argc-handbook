# Code of Conduct

ARGC holds its members to a single behavioral standard: act in a way that serves the collective and the mission. This means honest evaluations, reliable commitments, and direct communication when something is wrong. Members who consistently fall below this standard are reviewed — the process is documented, not informal.

## The Standard

ARGC is not a rules organization. We do not have a long list of prohibited behaviors because the standard is simple: your conduct should make the collective stronger, not weaker.

That said, some behaviors are specific enough to name.

## What Is Required

**Honest evaluations.** You score what you observed. Not what you want the relationship to look like. Not what the person asked for. What you actually saw.

**Reliable commitments.** If you say you will do something, you do it. If you cannot, you communicate that before the deadline — not after.

**Direct communication.** If something is wrong, you say it. To the right person, in writing, with specifics. Passive disengagement is not professionalism. It is avoidance.

**Respect for members' time.** Showing up late to scheduled evaluations, failing to respond to operational messages, and making others carry your accountability are all conduct failures.

## What Is Not Tolerated

- Deliberately inflating or deflating evaluation scores for social reasons
- Using the club's name or platform for personal gain unrelated to ARGC's mission
- Retaliating against a member for raising a legitimate concern through proper channels
- Sharing member data or platform records outside the club without authorization
- Any behavior that constitutes harassment, discrimination, or deliberate exclusion

This list is not exhaustive. The standard is "does this serve the collective?" If the honest answer is no, it is a conduct issue.

## Enforcement

Conduct issues are handled through the conflict resolution protocol. The escalation path is:
1. Direct resolution between parties
2. Node Leader review
3. Super Peer decision

Outcomes range from a documented warning to tier regression to removal from ARGC. Severity matches the issue.

No conduct decision is made informally. The process is documented, and the outcome is recorded on the platform.
