# Structure

ARGC is organized into nodes — small, domain-focused groups of engineers who share a defined area of responsibility. Above nodes sit Node Leaders, and above them Super Peers who oversee club-wide operations. Every layer exists to route accountability upward and output downward, not to create hierarchy for its own sake.

## The Node

The node is the primary unit of ARGC. It is small by design — large enough to cover a domain, small enough that everyone is visible.

Each node has:
- A defined domain (evaluations, platform, events, outreach, etc.)
- A Node Leader who carries accountability for the node's output
- Members who contribute within that domain and accumulate XP through doing so

You belong to a node before you belong to ARGC. Your node's output is your first accountability.

## Roles

| Role | Scope | Accountability |
|------|-------|----------------|
| **Member** | Within a node | Completes evaluations, attends events, accumulates XP |
| **Node Leader** | One node | Owns node output, manages internal operations, escalates issues upward |
| **Super Peer** | Club-wide | Oversees XP data, voting, cross-node operations, and club direction |

These are not titles. They are scopes of responsibility. A Node Leader who stops leading their node is reviewed. A Super Peer who stops operating at that level is reviewed.

## How Accountability Flows

Output is produced at the member level. Accountability for that output is carried by the Node Leader. Club-level accountability is carried by Super Peers.

This means:
- Members answer to their Node Leader for contribution activity
- Node Leaders answer to Super Peers for node output
- Super Peers answer to the documented standards of ARGC itself

There is no informal override. If a decision needs to be made, it follows this chain.

## What Does Not Have a Layer

Not everything needs a role. Administrative tasks, documentation, and platform maintenance are handled by whoever has the relevant skill and the node assignment — not by a dedicated bureaucratic tier.

ARGC does not create structure for its own sake. If a layer does not produce output, it does not exist.
